The BookWiz gallery brings the same writing studio to nine device settings. Five scenes follow a book from the library to the writing room, its story world, its cover and its page proof. The Watch and TV galleries follow their own companion features.

The visual language is warm paper, coral, butter yellow and ink. A folded open book forms the BookWiz **W**. Device frames, shadows, stationery and page shapes are original vector presentation artwork; interface text comes from BookWiz itself.

Phone, tablet and Watch case proportions follow the corresponding hardware dimensions. Screen artwork is fitted to its display ratio, with separate space for the illustrated status and navigation areas. Laptop frames retain the referenced display geometry; the keyboard decks use a simplified presentation perspective.

These are original hardware illustrations, not product photographs. Finishes, lighting, shadows and keyboard details are artistic treatments. Hardware names identify the devices being illustrated and do not imply endorsement.

## What the images show

**35 studio scenes** use current browser captures of the shared BookWiz interface. Android uses Ocean dark; iPhone uses Ivory light. Wider screens show the desktop navigation and workspace. The interface is the shared studio used by the native hosts, so these images are not claims of separate native screen implementations.

**10 companion scenes** are source-faithful previews reconstructed from the current Apple Watch and Apple TV SwiftUI layouts. They are labelled **Native interface preview**. They are not simulator or physical-device captures.

Book titles, authors, writing counts, model connection labels, saved continuation previews, transfer addresses and pairing codes are illustrative sample data. No private manuscript or account appears in the gallery. The Android and iPhone displays use different selected themes, while keeping the same writing tools.

## Image files

Each gallery contains five **1200 × 1600 PNGs**. Their typography and device artwork are baked into the images, so GitHub can display them without external fonts or scripts. The separate device downloads contain the complete images for that device.

The filenames describe the feature rather than a software release. The brand artwork and films also avoid release labels, so a new app version does not require a new visual identity.
