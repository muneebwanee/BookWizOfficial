# Workflows that produce usable pages

## An idea without an outline

Write the premise as a person facing a consequential choice, or a nonfiction question with a defined reader. Add a one-paragraph brief, the promise to the reader and the strongest uncertainty. Offer two or three distinct directions only when the idea is genuinely open. Explain the difference in stakes, structure or lens, not just labels.

Create a compact foundation before planning dozens of chapters. For fiction, establish the protagonist's desire, pressure, contradiction and possible change. For nonfiction, establish the thesis, boundaries, evidence standards and what the reader should be able to understand or do. Record accepted facts in `bookwiz.json` and explain provisional ideas in the planning files.

## Outline in useful units

Start with the opening, the irreversible middle turn and the ending's earned result. Expand into three to six chapters at a time. For each chapter record viewpoint, entry condition, active problem, turn, exit consequence, canon dependencies and a rough word budget. For nonfiction, substitute the chapter's question, claim, evidence, counterargument and reader takeaway.

Make every chapter do different work. Avoid repeated discovery scenes, interchangeable interviews or a list of topics without an argument. Preserve drafted chapter files when an outline changes. If a changed canon fact affects existing passages, list the affected chapter IDs rather than replacing their prose.

## A drafting session

Read the brief, accepted canon, previous chapter summary and current passage. The CLI's context command gathers this without contacting a provider. Work within the requested time, word band or passage boundary; one successful scene is more useful than an unattended whole-book loop.

Draft from the actual last line. Preserve an accepted prefix. Do not insert recap paragraphs merely to remind yourself what happened. Keep dialogue physically situated, give actions consequences and choose details that belong to this person and place. End where the agreed unit earns a turn; a cliffhanger is not required for every chapter.

If a provider rate-limits or truncates a reply, retain completed files, mark the current passage partial and record the exact next step. Resume from the completed foundation instead of generating the brief and outline again. Do not make repeated autonomous requests with no limit or fresh review.

## Nonfiction and research

Keep claims in an evidence ledger with source, date, quotation or location, verification state and intended chapter. A plausible citation is not evidence. Read supplied sources before quoting; clearly separate paraphrase, interpretation and original argument. Flag an unsupported claim in the draft rather than fabricating a reference. Do not invent interviews, testimonials or field observations.

## Several worlds or books

Give worlds and eras stable identifiers. Record which facts are global, world-specific, time-bound or known only to a viewpoint character. Keep a series ledger for changes accepted across books. An agent's brainstorming notes are not canonical facts until adopted. Use a separate project per manuscript and a plain shared canon file only when the writer wants it.

## Reviewing existing work

Read [revision](revision.md) for an editorial pass and [continuity](continuity.md) for contradiction tracking. Preserve an original before applying accepted revisions. A book-wide change should have a bounded plan: affected chapters, desired outcome, checks and a stopping point.

## Final preparation

Use [publication](publication.md). Report the saved word count, empty chapters and absent front matter as facts. The writer can choose to export a shorter or unfinished work; describe it accurately. Keep editable sources next to reader exports and retain an independent backup.
